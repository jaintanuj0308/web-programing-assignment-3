📜 Archival Synopsis & Lore

Set during the prosperous, hedonistic summer of 1922 in Long Island, New York, the novel is narrated by Nick Carraway, a Yale graduate and veteran who rents a modest bungalow adjacent to the colossal, marble-swathed mansion of the mysterious millionaire Jay Gatsby.

Gatsby throws lavish, champagne-drenched weekend soirees for hundreds of uninvited socialites, yet he remains aloof, staring across the dark waters of the bay toward a single green light gleaming at the end of Daisy Buchanan's dock. What follows is a tragic deconstruction of the American Dream, lost innocence, obsession, and the illusion of reinvention.

"So we beat on, boats against the current, borne back ceaselessly into the past."
— Nick Carraway, Chapter IX

The Great Gatsby // Chapter 1 (Excerpt)

In my younger and more vulnerable years my father gave me some advice that I've been turning over in my mind ever since.

"Whenever you feel like criticizing any one," he told me, "just remember that all the people in this world haven't had the advantages that you've had."

He didn't say any more, but we've always been unusually communicative in a reserved way, and I understood that he meant a great deal more than that. In consequence, I'm inclined to reserve all judgements, a habit that has opened up many curious natures to me and also made me the victim of not a few veteran bores.
